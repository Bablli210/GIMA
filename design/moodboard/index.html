MB-01 · Direction

Four directions, one recommendation

We developed four directions and reviewed each one twice internally: once from the point of view of a head of store development choosing a fit-out contractor, and once for craft, brand fit, speed and accessibility. Scores are out of 50 (five criteria, ten points each). The recommended direction keeps the best ideas from the other three.

MB-02 · Principles

Seven rules every page is checked against

The brand line asks for "a timeless visual language that does not chase trends. Confident without noise. Measured, exact and built to last." These rules turn that into decisions.

  • The store before the story

    A finished store in true colour, the one-line offer, brand names and a "Start a project" button are visible within a second, without scrolling. Drawings play over the work and never in front of it.

  • Show it once, then file it

    Design becoming build is shown once per project, on its cover and its preview. After that, every claim sits in a labelled field: client, mall, m², year, scope.

  • Rule of one

    At most one strong red element and one drawing device per screen, the logo excepted. Red marks a decision or a delivered scope, nothing else.

  • Say what we didn't do

    Each project lists who did what: "Concept: brand's global design", "Signage: by others". A contractor who is honest about scope is believed about the rest.

  • Empty is hidden, never faked

    No placeholder, dash or estimate ships. A field appears only when its data is verified. Twelve true projects beat forty half-empty rows.

  • Plain words, precise marks

    Navigation and headings use plain words: Work, Services, Process, About, Contact. The drafting language lives in the small marks. Numbers never count up.

  • Fast on a mid-range phone

    Many buyers will first open the link on a phone, often a mid-range Android. The largest image loads in under 2.5 seconds on 4G. Nothing hijacks scrolling. 3D loads only when asked for.

Which red is GIMA red?

The guideline states Sunset Red as #C63C32, but the logo artwork and every sample in the guideline render as #D72A27. We use #D72A27 so the website matches the logo files as supplied. Please check it against your signage and print with your sign-maker, and send the Pantone reference. If #C63C32 is correct, we change one value and every rule still holds.

Red on black is 4.0:1, so on black it is used for lines, marks and display type of 24px and up, never for small text.

MB-05 · Photography

Correct the colour, keep the finish

The archive photos come from different years and most carry a yellow tungsten cast. We correct each one once, so they sit together as a set while wood, leather, lacquer and lit glass still read true. Buyers judge finish, so photos where finish matters stay in colour.

How every photo is treated
  • Corrected once, at build time. White balance pulled toward neutral, yellows calmed, contrast lifted slightly. No live filters in the browser.
  • Colour where finish is judged: heroes, previews, elevations, detail shots.
  • Black and white only for backgrounds behind text and for filtered-out entries.
  • Keep the ceilings where bulkheads, coves and diffusers show infrastructure work.
  • Retouch out price tags, sale signs and neighbouring shopfronts reflected in glass.
  • Show each photo's year. Hiding dates looks worse than old dates.

MB-06 · Drawing marks

A small kit taken from the guideline

Every mark comes from GIMA's guideline or from standard architectural drafting. Each has one job and a fixed place, so the site feels drafted without dressing up as a blueprint.

  • Construction grid

    The logo's own guide lines. Used faintly behind the home hero and the portfolio index only.

  • 45° registration ticks

    Mark the corners of the hero photo, the index preview, the title block and the 3D stage, and appear on buttons on hover and focus. Nowhere else.

  • The 75° cut

    The angle of the guideline cover. The only diagonal cut on the site: image wipes, slab ends and the comparison divider.

  • Dimension line

    Carries figures such as m² and weeks. Link underlines borrow it: a rule with ticked ends.

  • Keynote tag

    Numbered hexagons point at what GIMA built on one elevation per project, with a written legend beside it.

  • Hidden line

    Dashed 4/4 for things above the ceiling or behind a finish, such as ducts and cable trays. Solid for visible edges.

MB-10 · 3D assembly

Yes to 3D, as evidence rather than decoration

We agree with the 3D idea, with one condition: the model must show what a photo cannot. Turnkey is a stack of layers, so the model assembles a store in build order, from the bare mall unit through ducts and ceilings to finishes, joinery and lights. A spinning model of a finished shop would prove nothing a photo doesn't. Below is a working placeholder built in code. Real projects replace it with the design team's own models.

[Placeholder model · real project model to follow]

This browser can't show the 3D view. In build order, the layers are shell, infrastructure, ceilings, finishes, joinery, and lighting for opening day.

Lighting & opening day

Lights on, snag list closed, keys handed over on the promised date.

Where it appears

  • One section on the home page, played as a scroll-linked image sequence, so no 3D engine is needed there.
  • Two or three flagship projects get an interactive model that loads only on tap, after the photos.
  • Every 3D moment ends on the real photograph of the trading store, taken from the same angle.

How it's built

  • three.js with white hairlines on dark planes, so the model looks like GIMA's drawings, not a glossy render.
  • Layers follow build order (shell, infrastructure, ceilings, finishes, joinery, lighting) and use the scope stack's trade names where the two overlap.
  • Under 2.5 MB per model on desktop and 1.2 MB on mobile. It never auto-rotates or loads above the fold.

What we need from GIMA

  • Original 3D design files for two or three flagships (SketchUp, 3ds Max, Revit or Rhino).
  • A trade-by-trade scope for each: what GIMA delivered and what the brand or others did.
  • The finished-store photo, plus any site photos of the bare shell at handover.

MB-11 · Motion

Mechanical, measured, rationed

Motion behaves like a drafting pen and a joiner's fit: it starts deliberately, lands exactly, and never bounces. Only four things move: the pen-then-fill trace, the 75° wipe, the scope slabs and the 3D build. Switch the header to "As-built" to see every page in its finished state.

  • Line draw

    cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1) · 600–800 ms · strokes 30 ms apart, in drafting order

  • Fill and wipe

    cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1) · 400–500 ms · slabs 120 ms apart

  • Hover and underline

    ease-out · 180 ms

  • 3D camera

    cubic-bezier(.65, 0, .35, 1) · 900–1200 ms

  • Each reveal plays once per visit. Content is on the page from the first frame; animation only plays over it.
  • One sequence per screen. On phones, only the card in the middle of the screen animates.
  • No count-ups, parallax or scroll hijacking. Figures sit still, like a record.
  • Reduced motion is respected. Visitors who ask for less motion get "As-built" by default.

MB-15 · What we avoid

The things that would make it generic, or untrue

  • Loaders, splash screens or pinned heroes that delay the first real store.
  • Yellow, uncorrected photos, and sepia or vintage filters. Black and white wherever finish is judged.
  • The drawing reveal on every image, or automatic "sketch filter" traces.
  • Architect cosplay: sheet codes as navigation, crosshair cursors, "approved" stamps, blueprint-blue backgrounds.
  • Claiming "designed by GIMA" on franchise work, or implying the brand was the client when it was a franchisee.
  • Invented counts, totals or on-time rates, and numbers that count up.
  • Red as a large field beyond the logo, red body text on black, two reds, red glows or gradients.
  • Rounded corners, glass effects, drop shadows, and diagonal cuts at any angle other than 75°.
  • 3D above the fold, auto-rotating models, game-style walkthroughs, glossy photoreal renders.
  • Custom cursors, cursor trails, parallax, smooth-scroll hijacking, scrambled text.
  • Stock or AI-generated interiors shown as GIMA work.
  • Retyping the GIMA wordmark in a font. The logo is always the supplied artwork.
  • Icon grids for services, and carousels for the portfolio.
